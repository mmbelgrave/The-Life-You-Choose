Something needs to change. But I don't know what yet.

Updated 18 November 2026 · about 4 minutes to read

I had a good job and a comfortable home. Still, I kept feeling that something was missing.

A book and a programme helped me put words to what I was feeling. I still found questions about passion and purpose hard to answer. Looking back, I wish I'd started with a smaller question: when did I notice that I wanted something to be different?

This short exercise helps you find that moment. You do not need to know what you want. You only need to bring back one moment.

In short

  • You do not need to know what you want to start.
  • Start with one moment when you noticed that you wanted something different.
  • Finish one sentence and write down one question today.
  • See this week whether your question still fits.
  • You can do all of it yourself. If you want to talk about it, use the advice further down this guide on how to ask.

Why one moment is enough

Big questions such as "What is my passion?" or "What is my purpose?" are hard to answer on a blank page. A moment is concrete. You know where you were, what had just happened and what you felt. You can work with that, even if you have no idea yet what you want.

The exercise in three steps

Take a piece of paper or open the notes app on your phone.

  1. Bring back one moment. When did you last think that you wanted something different? Write down where you were and what had just happened.
  2. Finish the sentence. "At that moment I wished that…" Write down whatever comes up. "I do not know yet" is also an answer.
  3. Turn it into one question. Read back what you wrote. What would you like to understand better? Write down one question. If the feeling comes back this week, note what happened just before it and see whether your question still fits.
Made-up example (Erik).

The moment. After dinner, Erik sees the chair he started restoring.

The sentence. He finishes the sentence: "At that moment I would have liked to work on that chair a little longer."

His question. "What would I like to make more often, simply because I enjoy it?"

If you want to talk about it

You can do this guide entirely on your own. If you do want to share it with someone, a partner, a friend or someone you trust, this sentence can be a start:

"I notice this keeps coming back. I do not know yet what I want to do with it, but would you listen for a moment?"

You do not need to know what you want to say this.
