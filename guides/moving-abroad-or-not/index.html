Moving abroad or not? Start with an ordinary day.

Updated 21 October 2026 · about 6 minutes to read

Are you wondering whether to move abroad? Then you are taking something seriously. But "move or not" is often too big a question to answer in one go. It helps to ask a smaller one first: what life do I actually want, and what in my ordinary week does not fit it?

I have built a new life abroad twice, first in Bali and then in Portugal. I am not writing this to talk you into one thing or the other. I am writing it to give you an order in which to think.

In short

  • Do not start with the country. Start with your ordinary week.
  • Write down one ordinary day in the life you want, detailed enough to test.
  • See what that day needs: a different week where you are now, a different place, or waiting a little longer.
  • Test something small before you decide anything big.

Away from something, or towards something?

A lot of doubt begins with "I want out of here". That feeling is real. But it does not yet say where you want to go, or whether a move would fix it.

Sometimes what bothers you sits in your working week, in evenings that fill up, in too little time outside, or in a decision you have been putting off for a long time. A new country does not remove those things by itself. Sometimes a move is the answer: when you miss what is only possible somewhere else, such as another climate, more space or another language around you. It is worth making that distinction before you sell, cancel or arrange anything.

Five questions to start with

  1. What in my ordinary week do I no longer want? Think of hours, evenings, people and places, not of a country.
  2. What do I definitely want to keep? People, work, habits, a place you know. This is what a change has to leave standing.
  3. What would I change if I could arrange the same week here? Fewer working days, a fixed day off, a different way of working, more time outside.
  4. What is only possible somewhere else? This is the question that truly justifies a move, or does not.
  5. What can I test in a small way before I decide big? A trial period, an exploratory visit, a conversation with someone who already lives it.

Write your answers down. Written answers can be read back and compared later. In your head they change a little every time.

Write down one ordinary day

A holiday tells you little, because most days are ordinary days. So choose an ordinary Tuesday in the life you want. Start when you get up and walk through the day until the evening. Include the small things: when you work, who is around you, where you are outside, what you do not enjoy but comes with it.

The more concrete the day, the better you can test it. "A house with a view" is too vague to plan. "Coffee on the terrace before anyone else is awake" is something you can try out.

Made-up example. I work four days. On Friday I am outside, even when it rains. My evenings are mine: two evenings a week work ends at six. My sister lives ten minutes away and comes for dinner on Thursday.

What your day tells you

Read your day back and see what it needs. Usually it comes down to one of these three:

What you noticeWhat that could mean
You mainly miss time, calm or a different rhythmA change where you are now may be enough: different working days, free evenings, more time outside. Test that first, because it is smaller and cheaper.
You miss things that are not possible hereThen looking further makes sense. Test a place against your ordinary day, not against a list of advantages.
You do not know yetWaiting is also a decision. Set a date to look again, so that it does not turn into drifting.
Made-up example of staying. Daan writes down his ordinary Tuesday and sees that he does not miss another country, but his free mornings and his evenings. He asks his employer for a four-week trial with one day off a week. At the end he looks again: does the week feel different, and what does it cost him? That way he makes a small change where he is, and keeps moving as a question for later.

All three are real outcomes. Staying is not failing and leaving is not running away, as long as you know why you do it.

Test small, and go as a tester, not as a tourist

Before you decide anything big, you can run a small experiment. Where you are now: try a different working week for four weeks, or two evenings a week without work. Somewhere else: do not go on holiday, go as a tester. Do an ordinary day of shopping, see how long it takes to get to a doctor, find out what it is like when it rains.

I travelled through Portugal for two months in a camper and looked at 25 plots of land. I felt connected to one region and not at all to another. I could only find that out on the spot.

When you need a professional

This is not legal, tax or financial advice. Rules on tax, pensions, health insurance and residence differ per country and change. If you do move, check the current rules with the official source. For Dutch citizens a good start is the NederlandWereldwijd checklist (in Dutch). For tax, pensions and residence law, ask a qualified adviser.
